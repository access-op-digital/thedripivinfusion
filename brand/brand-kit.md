# The Drip IV Infusion — Brand Visual Identity Kit

**Website analyzed:** [thedripivinfusion.com](https://thedripivinfusion.com/)
**Analysis scope:** Live homepage visual system, messaging, UI styling, imagery, typography, and service presentation.
**Reference capture:** [Homepage visual reference](./homepage-reference.webp)

> **Brand essence:** Nurse-led IV hydration and wellness care that feels professional, calm, convenient, and human—delivered either at home or in a comfortable Gilbert office.

## 1. Brand Personality

| Attribute | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Brand Type** | Clinical wellness with approachable, mobile-service convenience |
| **Industry** | Mobile and in-office IV hydration, infusion, and wellness therapy |
| **Target Audience** | Arizona residents seeking convenient hydration, recovery, energy, symptom support, and wellness care at home, work, hotel, or in-office |
| **Overall Vibe** | Trustworthy, calm, professional, caring, accessible |
| **Core Positioning** | Licensed nurses bring expert IV therapy directly to the client, with safety, responsiveness, and comfort emphasized throughout the experience |

The brand balances **medical credibility** with **wellness ease**. It is not cold or hospital-like, and it is not overly luxurious or spa-like; the strongest visual territory is clean clinical care softened by human service and comfortable lifestyle imagery.

## 2. Color Palette

The colors below are the active brand colors observed in the page components. The site also contains unused WordPress preset colors; those should not be treated as part of the brand palette.

| Color role | Hex code | Recommended usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deep Navy** | `#153060` | Hero fields, primary CTAs, dark headings, FAQ panels |
| **Muted Medical Blue** | `#5A79AD` | Secondary headings, section labels, emphasis words, supporting accents |
| **Pale Aqua** | `#B5DEF5` | Hero highlight text, light buttons, soft UI accents, calm backgrounds |
| **Clinical Teal** | `#13646D` | Secondary CTAs, action highlights, conversion moments |
| **Soft Gray** | `#F6F6F6` | Alternating section backgrounds, treatment-menu and service-area bands |
| **White** | `#FFFFFF` | Main content backgrounds, body copy on dark fields, clean negative space |
| **Black** | `#000000` | Body copy and headings on light backgrounds |
| **Footer Sky Blue** | `#5DBBE9` | Footer or large supporting brand field; use sparingly outside the footer |

### Color rules

- Use **Deep Navy** as the dominant anchor color; it communicates reliability and medical confidence.
- Use **Muted Medical Blue** for secondary emphasis rather than bright blue.
- Use **Pale Aqua** to create softness and breathability, not as a loud accent.
- Use **Clinical Teal** for action-oriented moments and selected conversion buttons.
- Keep most compositions white, navy, or soft gray. Avoid introducing unrelated gradients or saturated rainbow colors.
- Maintain strong contrast for medical information and accessibility. Pale Aqua should not carry small black or white text without checking contrast.

## 3. Typography

| Element | Font | Style and usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Display and headings** | **Questrial** | Regular weight, geometric rounded sans-serif, clean and contemporary; use for H1–H3 headlines and short emphasis lines |
| **Body copy** | **FreightSansProBook-Regular** | Light book-weight sans-serif with an editorial, human feel; use for paragraphs, descriptions, navigation, and supporting information |
| **CTA and utility labels** | Freight Sans or a neutral sans-serif | Uppercase, semibold, approximately 1px letter spacing; use for booking actions and short interface labels |
| **Review widget exception** | Poppins appears in the embedded Trustindex review component | Treat as a third-party widget style, not as a core brand font |

### Typographic hierarchy

- Headlines are large, airy, and mostly regular-weight rather than heavy or condensed.
- The page frequently contrasts **black or white lead words** with **Muted Medical Blue** emphasis words.
- Use short all-caps eyebrow labels such as `IV TREATMENT MENU`, `SIMPLE PROCESS`, and `OUR LOCATIONS` in small blue text.
- Use sentence case for educational and service descriptions. Reserve all caps for labels and CTAs.
- Avoid script, serif, novelty, ultra-bold, or tightly compressed display fonts.

## 4. Visual Design Patterns

| Design element | Observed / recommended style |
| --- | --- |
| **Visuals used** | Documentary photography of nurses, clients, IV setup, and care environments; limited decorative graphics |
| **Icon style** | Simple line or minimal medical/service icons, such as shield, clock, and star trust markers |
| **Shape language** | Clean rectangles with gently rounded corners; approximately 10px radius on primary content CTAs and image frames |
| **Whitespace** | Generous; use breathing room around headings, service cards, and conversion areas |
| **Borders and dividers** | Minimal borders; thin separators and subtle tonal changes are preferred over decorative rules |
| **Shadow usage** | Very restrained; use soft elevation only when separating cards from white backgrounds |
| **Layout** | Wide, responsive sections; two-column image/text blocks; centered section introductions; repeated conversion bands |
| **Content rhythm** | Alternate white and soft-gray bands, then use Deep Navy for high-confidence process and final conversion sections |
| **Buttons** | Flat, high-contrast fills; Deep Navy or Clinical Teal; white label text; rounded corners; uppercase with breathing room |

## 5. Image Style on Existing Content

- **Photo style:** Authentic, documentary lifestyle photography rather than stock-photo gloss. Show nurses administering IV therapy, real client care, mobile visits, and professional in-office settings.
- **Subjects:** Licensed nurses, adult clients, hands and arms during setup, clinical supplies, hydration equipment, and calm home or office environments.
- **Lighting:** Bright, soft, natural or diffused indoor light. Keep skin tones natural and the overall image high-key.
- **Color treatment:** Neutral whites, soft grays, black clothing or equipment, and occasional cool blue/aqua environmental cues. Avoid heavy color grading.
- **Composition:** Rounded-corner portraits and landscape crops; balanced two-column layouts; leave clean negative space where text may be placed.
- **Text on images:** Minimal. Keep meaningful service explanations in the layout beside or below the photo rather than covering faces or medical equipment.
- **Filters and overlays:** Use a subtle navy overlay only when needed for legibility. Do not apply dramatic cinematic filters, neon effects, or glossy beauty retouching.

## 6. Brand Elements

- **Logo:** The wordmark reads **THE DRIP** in a geometric navy uppercase style, with a smaller `iv infusion` descriptor beneath. Preserve the original proportions, clear space, and navy/pale-aqua color relationship.
- **Recurring motifs:** IV tubing, controlled drip imagery, nurse-client care, hydration, calm recovery, location convenience, shield/safety cues, and the Arizona service footprint.
- **Trust signals:** Licensed RN staff, response-time claims, star-rating cues, safety protocols, pharmaceutical-grade ingredients, and clear service-area information.
- **Brand language:** Direct, reassuring, and action-led. Common themes include `feel better`, `recover faster`, `without the wait`, `wherever you are`, `safety`, `standards`, `comfort`, and `trusted care`.
- **Emotional territory:** Relief, reassurance, restored energy, convenience, and professional care without pressure.

## 7. Blog Content Image Guidelines

### Purpose and context

Blog visuals should educate and reassure while making IV therapy feel understandable and approachable. They should explain treatment processes, recovery contexts, service areas, safety, and wellness routines without looking like a technical hospital brochure.

### Color usage

- Anchor educational visuals in **Deep Navy `#153060`**, **Muted Medical Blue `#5A79AD`**, **Pale Aqua `#B5DEF5`**, white, and soft gray.
- Clinical Teal `#13646D` may highlight one key step, statistic, or callout.
- Allow a small amount of natural skin tone, warm home interior color, or muted desert daylight in photography.
- Do not use loud red, neon green, purple, or candy-bright colors unless medically necessary for a diagram and kept subordinate.

### Typography

- Use Questrial for short educational titles and step headings.
- Use Freight Sans for explanations, labels, and captions.
- Text is allowed and encouraged when it clarifies a process; keep it concise and highly legible.
- Use navy or black text on light backgrounds and white text only on navy/teal panels.

### Composition and layout

- Prefer vertical step flows, three-part process diagrams, simple comparison layouts, and editorial photo-plus-copy compositions.
- Use generous whitespace, clean alignment, and rounded information cards.
- Combine documentary photography with minimal line icons or restrained diagrams.
- Keep faces, hands, tubing, and equipment unobstructed by text overlays.

1. A three-step infographic explaining booking, nurse screening, and the IV session using navy headings, pale-aqua step cards, simple shield/clock/IV icons, and a clean white background.
2. A lifestyle editorial image of a nurse preparing a calm at-home IV visit, with soft natural light, a neutral living-room setting, and an open navy text area for a short educational headline.
3. A recovery guide showing hydration, rest, and follow-up as a horizontal sequence with documentary imagery, small teal accents, and a restrained Arizona wellness feel.

### What to avoid for blog content

- Neon palettes, rainbow gradients, cartoon characters, or playful wellness clichés.
- Dense paragraphs pasted onto images, tiny labels, and cluttered multi-column layouts.
- Fear-based illness imagery or absolute treatment promises.
- Cold, sterile blue-only compositions with no human warmth.

## 8. Business Offerings / Products / Services Image Guidelines

### Purpose and context

Service visuals should make booking feel safe, easy, and worthwhile. They should show the quality of the nurse-led experience and the convenience of receiving IV therapy at home, at work, at a hotel, or in the Gilbert office.

### Exact color palette

- **Primary:** Deep Navy `#153060` for hero backgrounds, main CTAs, FAQ panels, and high-trust sections.
- **Secondary:** Muted Medical Blue `#5A79AD` for supporting headings and service emphasis.
- **Soft accent:** Pale Aqua `#B5DEF5` for light buttons, highlights, and calm environmental cues.
- **Action accent:** Clinical Teal `#13646D` for selected booking and availability CTAs.
- **Backgrounds:** White `#FFFFFF` and Soft Gray `#F6F6F6`.
- **Restrictions:** Avoid saturated red, neon green, purple, orange, black-on-black, and glossy metallic palettes that move the brand away from clinical wellness.

### Typography

- Use short Questrial-style headings with strong size contrast and regular weight.
- Use Freight Sans for brief service descriptions and supporting details.
- Keep promotional image text minimal: service name, one benefit-oriented line, and one CTA at most.
- Place text in clean negative space or a solid navy/teal panel, never over a client's face or treatment area.

### Premium styling and composition

- **Lighting:** Soft, directional daylight or diffused clinical lighting; clean but not overexposed.
- **Backgrounds:** Bright home, hotel, office, or calm treatment-room environments with minimal clutter.
- **Depth of field:** Medium or shallow focus to keep the nurse/client interaction or treatment equipment primary.
- **Placement:** Use human-centered asymmetry or a balanced two-column composition; keep the subject slightly off-center when text is needed.
- **Props:** IV bag, clean tubing, gloves, sanitizer, tablet or booking device, neutral chair, soft blanket, and understated wellness details.
- **Material language:** Clean medical surfaces, soft textiles, white walls, brushed neutral hardware, and subtle aqua/navy cues, not marble-and-gold luxury clichés.

2. A calm in-office hydration session showing professional equipment, comfortable seating, and a relaxed adult client, framed with white space and a pale-aqua accent.
3. A treatment-menu composition with three neat service cards (Hydration, Energy, and Recovery) using simple icons, black text, navy headings, and a single teal CTA.

### What to avoid for products/services

- Overly luxurious spa imagery, gold marble, champagne, silk, or beauty-product styling.
- Dark, moody, high-contrast photography that makes treatment look intimidating.
- Cartoon, anime, 3D-rendered, futuristic, or hyper-tech visual treatments.
- Excessive medical close-ups, visible blood, painful needle moments, or anxious expressions.
- Busy props, messy homes, crowded clinical rooms, or generic stock-photo smiles.
- Text-heavy promotional graphics and unverified medical promises.
- Unrelated gradients, saturated warm colors, and cool electric-blue cyber aesthetics.

## Implementation checklist

- Use the original logo asset and preserve its navy/pale-aqua relationship.
- Treat `#153060`, `#5A79AD`, `#B5DEF5`, and `#13646D` as the controlled action palette.
- Keep Questrial and Freight Sans visually distinct: display versus body.
- Use real, calm, nurse-led photography as the default visual proof point.
- Keep educational visuals more explanatory than promotional; keep booking visuals more focused and text-light.
- Check accessibility contrast whenever using Pale Aqua or Muted Medical Blue for small text.
- Avoid treating the embedded review widget's Poppins font or third-party Google review graphics as core brand identity.
