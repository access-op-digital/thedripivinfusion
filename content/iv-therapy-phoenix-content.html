Do You Need a Prescription for IV Therapy in Arizona?

Yes. Arizona requires a valid order from a licensed prescriber before a nurse may run an elective IV hydration or nutrient infusion, and that requirement holds whether the drip runs in a clinic or in your Phoenix living room.

The Arizona State Board of Nursing sets the terms in its advisory opinion on IV hydration and other therapies, read alongside A.A.C. R4-19-402. A registered nurse may administer IV fluids, nutrients and medications only under an order written by a Licensed Provider with prescriptive authority, meaning a physician, physician assistant or advanced practice registered nurse acting inside their own scope. The order must be individualised to the patient and carry a medical rationale, so a generic menu selection does not satisfy it.

A good faith exam comes before the order. The Board's position is that issuing standing orders for elective IV therapy, for a nurse or other staff member to follow, does not meet the prescriber's duty to the patient, so a provider who signs a blanket authorisation for every customer who walks in has not met the standard.

The nurse's own limits are drawn just as clearly. It is outside an Arizona RN or LPN scope of practice to independently order, compound or prescribe IV fluids, IV medications or IV therapeutic regimens, or to exercise independent medical judgment or medical diagnosis. Arizona also expects an IV hydration business that does not operate through a private medical office or clinic to retain a licensed medical professional with full practice authority as its medical director.

For the patient this mostly means paperwork happens before the needle: intake, exam, order, then infusion. Related reading: IV therapy prescription requirements, who can administer IV therapy and IV therapy regulations.
