Do You Need a Prescription for IV Therapy in Arizona?

Yes. Arizona requires a valid order from a licensed prescriber before a nurse may run an elective IV hydration or nutrient infusion, whether in a clinic or in your Phoenix living room.

Under A.A.C. R4-19-402 and the Arizona State Board of Nursing advisory opinion, the order must come from a physician, PA or APRN, be individualised to you, and carry a medical rationale. A generic menu selection doesn't satisfy it.

A good faith exam comes before the order; blanket standing orders don't meet the standard. For you, paperwork happens before the needle: intake, exam, order, then infusion.

Related: prescription requirements, who can administer IV therapy, IV therapy regulations.

What an Arizona Nurse Can and Can't Do

It is outside an Arizona RN or LPN scope to independently order, compound or prescribe IV fluids, medications or regimens, or to make an independent medical diagnosis. Anything outside the order goes back to the prescriber.

Arizona also expects an IV hydration business operating outside a private medical office or clinic to retain a licensed medical professional with full practice authority as its medical director.

What Happens During Your Phoenix IV Appointment

Eight steps from booking call to follow-up. About 45 to 60 minutes once the nurse arrives.

Step 1 - Book

Call or text (602) 341-3511, email hello@thedripivinfusion.com, or book online. Lines are staffed 7:00AM to 9:00PM, seven days a week.

Step 2 - Health intake

A health history covering medications, allergies, pregnancy status and prior reactions. Arizona's required good faith exam and individualised order are set up here.

Step 3 - Nurse arrives

A licensed RN travels to your home, office or hotel, typically within 60 minutes. Coverage spans the 85003-85020 ZIPs and the surrounding metro.

Step 4 - Vitals and consult

Blood pressure, pulse and temperature, reviewed against your order. Anything outside the order goes back to the prescriber.

Step 5 - Drip selection

Confirm the drip and any $30 add-ons against the order on file. The eight bags run $195 to $495.

Step 6 - Infusion, 30 to 60 minutes

The nurse places the catheter, starts the infusion and stays with you throughout. Sit, work or lie down; the line runs the same.

Step 7 - Post-care check

Catheter removed, vitals rechecked, no site pain, dizziness or swelling confirmed. Sharps and used supplies leave with the nurse.

Step 8 - Follow-up

A text or call confirms how you responded and logs anything for next time. After-hours questions go to (602) 341-3511.
